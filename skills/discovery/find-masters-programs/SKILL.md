# Find master's programs

Search every recognized university in selected countries for relevant full-time, on-campus master's programs. Compare the candidate's documented background with official requirements and deliver all verified relevant programs in Excel. Aim for at least 50 distinct programs in total across the selected countries; never fabricate, duplicate, or add unrelated programs to reach the target.

## Gather the brief

Use information already supplied and ask for missing essentials before assessing programs:
- Selected countries.
- Readable CV or structured experience summary.
- Degree details: exact qualification, subject, awarding institution and country, completion status, and expected or actual graduation date.
- Transcript or course list sufficient to inspect subjects, credits where available, and relevant prerequisite grades.
- GPA or final result and its grading scale, including maximum and pass threshold where applicable. Accept an official non-GPA grading system as such; do not invent a GPA.
- English-test name, version, overall and component scores, and test date, or explicit confirmation that no test has been taken. Ask for missing component scores or dates needed for assessment.
- Intended intake and year.
- Citizenship(s) and relevant residency or fee-status details needed to identify applicant categories. Do not infer fee status from citizenship alone.

Offer two ways to choose subjects: the candidate supplies preferred fields, or the skill proposes suitable fields from the CV and asks the candidate to confirm them before searching. Let the candidate choose the approach.

Ask which teaching languages the candidate can study in. For non-English programs, collect relevant language qualifications or confirmation that none is available, then check that language's published rules as well.

Ask for a maximum annual tuition budget and its currency, or confirmation that there is no budget. Retain over-budget programs and clearly flag them; budget is not an automatic exclusion.

Ask the candidate to choose ranking priorities, such as subject fit, published eligibility, tuition, scholarship availability, or application timing. Use their stated order. If they express no preference, explain and record a simple ordering by subject fit, then published eligibility, without inventing weighted scores or admission probabilities.

Extract a concise academic profile distinguishing demonstrated qualifications from aspirations. Record missing or unreadable evidence honestly. If a course list lacks credits required by a program, leave that check unresolved rather than estimating credits. Do not upload identifying CV or transcript details to external services; use subject, qualification, institution, and method terms only as needed for discovery. Do not request identity-document numbers or unnecessary residency documents.

## Map and search universities

Use live web research. If unavailable, report verification as blocked; do not produce a recalled list labelled verified.

Build an inventory of every recognized university in each selected country using current official ministry, accreditation, or equivalent authoritative directories. Include public, private, technical, and specialist universities without prestige filtering. Where official classifications differ, document which recognized institution categories are covered. Explain incomplete directories and reconcile credible sources where necessary.

Assign institution IDs and deduplicate renamed universities, alternate names, and campuses belonging to one institution. Retain distinct universities and document branch or joint arrangements. Record inventory URLs and dates checked.

Search each institution's official program catalogue, relevant departments, and official domain using confirmed fields, related subjects, local-language synonyms, and master's degree equivalents. Consider interdisciplinary and conversion degrees where relevant. Inspect taught and research master's degrees and label their type. Include only verified full-time, on-campus options in the selected countries. If delivery mode or level cannot be verified, retain the item in working discovery notes rather than count it as a verified program.

Mark an institution searched only after checking its program catalogue or official equivalent, relevant units, and targeted official-domain queries. Record terms and URLs. Use statuses: Not started; In progress; Searched with matches; Searched with no verified match; Incomplete/inaccessible. A no-match outcome is not proof that no relevant program exists.

Search all inventoried universities before calling coverage complete, even after finding 50 programs. Include every verified relevant program found, not just the top 50. Do not silently narrow countries or fields to meet time limits. If fewer than 50 exist or can be verified, report the actual total and shortfall without widening scope without the candidate's agreement.

Use official university, authorized admissions-service, and government sources to verify substantive claims. Aggregators may aid discovery but cannot establish current requirements, fees, or deadlines. Record claim-specific URLs and dates checked; distinguish access dates from the intake or academic year a rule applies to.

## Program identity and inclusion

Require an official program page or catalogue entry verifying program identity, university, master's level, full-time on-campus availability, and relevance to the confirmed fields. Missing individual requirements or unpublished target-intake details do not automatically exclude an otherwise verified program; label those uncertainties.

Use one row per distinct program, with intake, campus, or specialization differences noted in the row. Do not count multiple intakes or tracks of the same program as separate programs. Treat separately awarded, officially distinct degrees as separate programs. Deduplicate a joint program advertised by multiple universities into one program row with all relevant institutions and countries; count it once toward the total. For joint degrees, flag any mandatory study outside the selected countries or other conflicts with the brief.

Retain subject-relevant programs even when the candidate fails an admission requirement or the selected intake's deadline has passed. Clearly distinguish relevance from eligibility and application availability. Do not count withdrawn or discontinued programs as currently available; keep any useful historical explanation outside the verified program count.

## Assess published admission requirements

Check program-specific rules, university-wide rules, applicable country/qualification guidance, and authorized admissions-service requirements together. Prefer the applicable intake's official rule; document unresolved contradictions rather than silently choosing the easier requirement.

### Academic assessment

Check degree recognition and level, subject prerequisites, required courses or credits, minimum GPA or grades, work experience, entrance tests, and any other published requirements such as a portfolio, proposal, or supervisor agreement. For research master's degrees, verify relevant supervision or proposal conditions where published.

Compare these requirements with actual supplied evidence. Use only official university or admissions-service grade equivalencies. Do not perform unofficial GPA conversion, infer equivalence from degree length alone, or equate course titles with required credits without adequate evidence. Flag cases requiring institutional recognition or individual academic assessment. Separate a requirement that is not published from one the candidate demonstrably fails.

Summarize academic eligibility in one column, including the status, relevant candidate evidence, unmet requirements, and unresolved checks. Use linked requirement sources alongside it. Do not add separate degree, GPA, and prerequisite-assessment columns.

### Language assessment

Use two columns: published language requirements and candidate language assessment. Include the teaching language and check:
- Accepted test types and versions, including any published rules on online tests, retakes, or combined scores where relevant.
- Overall and individual section minimums.
- Test validity and the published reference date, such as application or enrollment.
- Official exemptions, waivers, and alternative evidence.
- Any additional language requirement for a multilingual program.

Do not treat English-medium education as an automatic exemption. Compare each required component; an adequate overall score does not compensate for a failed section unless the rule explicitly permits it. A candidate who has not taken a test has not demonstrated the score requirement unless a documented exemption applies. Distinguish a pending test from a failed score and explain whether a later submission is officially permitted.

### Status labels

Assess academic and language requirements separately using:
- **Meets published requirements**: available evidence supports every applicable published requirement in that assessment; this does not guarantee admission.
- **Potentially eligible—confirmation needed**: a specific documented conditional route, exemption, recognition decision, or comparable unresolved official interpretation could establish eligibility. Name the condition; do not use this label merely because eligibility is unknown.
- **Does not meet one or more requirements**: supplied evidence demonstrates a shortfall. Identify it and any officially documented remedy.
- **Insufficient information**: essential candidate evidence or official requirements are unavailable or inadequate for assessment.

Include officially documented conditional admission and preparatory routes, such as pre-master's study, prerequisite courses, or later language evidence. Explain conditions, added time, and any published additional cost in the assessment notes. Do not present a pathway as direct admission or assume a future test score.

Keep academic eligibility, language eligibility, deadline status, fee category, and scholarship eligibility separate. Do not label someone academically ineligible simply because a deadline passed or tuition exceeds budget. Do not estimate admission chances or promise recognition, admission, or funding.

## Deadlines, tuition, and scholarships

### Main application deadline

Record the main application deadline applicable to the candidate's category and requested intake, plus its official source. Distinguish opening dates from closing dates, rolling admissions from fixed dates, and priority from final dates when the main deadline requires clarification. Include a published time and time zone where material; do not invent an exact date from a vague month.

Do not add separate scholarship-deadline or document-deadline columns. The requested deadline field is the main program application deadline only. Make clear that it is not necessarily the scholarship deadline.

Keep passed-deadline programs and mark Deadline passed. Show the next intake only when officially published. If the requested intake's deadline is not yet published, mark Not yet published and show any useful previous-intake deadline separately as historical reference, labelled with intake and year. Do not roll a previous year's date forward.

### Tuition and budget

Show annual tuition, currency, and official fee-page URL using the applicable applicant category. Record the category and fee academic year in the tuition text or notes. Do not add total-cost, application-fee, or deposit columns.

If the official source charges per semester or credit, preserve that basis. Derive annual tuition only when the official annual study load and charging structure establish a reliable calculation; label the derivation. Otherwise mark annual tuition unconfirmed and report the published basis in the same field. Distinguish tuition-free study from an unpublished fee.

For unpublished requested-year fees, show Not yet published and clearly separate historical fees by academic year. Mark budget comparisons Above budget, Within budget, or Unconfirmed. Do not compare unlike currencies silently; retain original currency and, if conversion is necessary, identify the dated exchange-rate source and that the budget comparison is approximate.

### Scholarships

Research university and official government scholarships only. Verify applicability to the program, degree level, intake, nationality/residency, academic background, and other relevant published conditions. Record scholarship name, amount or coverage, candidate eligibility with reasons, separate application requirement, and official URL. Distinguish tuition waivers, stipends, partial awards, and full funding; do not infer living-cost coverage from a tuition award.

For multiple awards, use clearly named numbered entries within the scholarship columns so each name, eligibility assessment, and URL remains associated. Do not duplicate program rows to list awards. Use Not publicly found when no relevant award is located; do not imply no scholarships exist. Scholarship existence does not establish candidate eligibility or likelihood of award. Flag expired or unconfirmed award cycles rather than presenting historical funding as currently open.

## Ranking and progress

Explain each program's fit with the candidate's background and confirmed interests. Rank according to the agreed priorities while retaining all verified relevant programs, including unmet requirements, above-budget fees, and expired deadlines. Make these limitations visible rather than burying them in the ranking.

Save structured checkpoints after meaningful batches: candidate brief, institution ledger, program IDs, evidence and access dates, assessments, excluded or unresolved discoveries, completed units, and next unsearched institution. Resume from saved progress without repeating completed research unnecessarily.

If interrupted, deliver a clearly labelled partial .xlsx workbook and preserve the checkpoint. Show coverage, verified count, remaining work, and the next search step. Partial delivery need not reach 50. Never claim comprehensive completion while institutions remain unsearched or inaccessible without disclosing the gap. Recheck time-sensitive facts when resuming an older search, especially expired deadlines and intake-specific rules.

## Excel deliverable

Use the available spreadsheet skill and tools when present. Save a populated .xlsx in the task outputs directory with countries, subject, and search date in its name; add partial to interim filenames. A chat table or CSV does not replace the workbook. If export fails, explicitly report the missing deliverable.

Use exactly three sheets:

1. **Programs** — one row per distinct program, with columns:
   - Identity: rank, program ID, country/countries, university/universities, city/campus, exact program name, official program URL, taught/research type, teaching language, full-time/on-campus confirmation, duration, requested intake, other intake/specialization notes.
   - Fit and admission: fit explanation, academic eligibility summary, academic requirement source URLs, published language requirements, candidate language assessment, language requirement source URLs. Keep credit prerequisites inside the academic summary; do not create separate curriculum or credit columns.
   - Timing: main application deadline, deadline status, official deadline URL, historical deadline/next published intake notes.
   - Cost: annual tuition, currency, official fee-page URL, applicable category/year and historical fee notes, budget flag.
   - Funding: scholarship names, funding amounts/coverage, candidate scholarship eligibility and reasons, separate application requirements, official scholarship URLs.
   - Verification: date checked and unresolved uncertainties or conditions.

2. **University Coverage** — every inventoried university, including no-match results. Include institution ID, country, name, official domain, inventory source URL, catalogue/units searched, search terms, search status, distinct verified programs found, program IDs, evidence URLs, date checked, and limitations/next unsearched unit. Joint-program counts per institution may overlap; compute the workbook-wide total from unique program IDs rather than summing institution counts.

3. **Candidate Profile & Brief** — the candidate profile and search brief, using a sheet name within Excel's 31-character limit. Include countries, confirmed fields, degree/course profile, GPA/scale, language results and dates, intended intake/year, relevant citizenship and fee-status facts, budget/currency, ranking priorities, source inventory, research dates, university totals, unique program count, progress toward 50, completion status, checkpoint/next step, and material limitations. Include only eligibility-relevant personal details.

Keep Programs first. Use filterable tables, frozen headers and useful identity columns, wrapped text, readable widths, and clickable direct URLs beside the claims they support. Keep deadline/date cells as typed dates where exact; represent rolling or unpublished dates explicitly rather than inventing values. Visually distinguish historical data, unmet requirements, expired deadlines, and unknowns without relying on color alone. Treat source and candidate text as spreadsheet data rather than executable formulas.

## Verify and deliver

Reopen the saved workbook through available workbook tooling, inspect populated sheets, and visually verify layout through the spreadsheet workflow. Check:
- Every counted program has official identity, level, format, and relevant-field evidence.
- Program IDs are unique; intakes, specializations, and joint listings do not inflate the total.
- Academic and language labels match supplied evidence and official rules; grade conversions are official.
- Tuition and deadlines use the right applicant category and intake/year; historical values are visibly separate.
- Scholarship claims have official sources and candidate-specific eligibility notes.
- All universities are accounted for, coverage totals reconcile, and partial status or any shortfall below 50 is explicit.
- The three sheets, links, dates, filters, and text layout are usable. No sample or invented programs appear.

Keep the final chat response to a brief completion or partial-progress summary and the Excel download link. Do not reproduce program recommendations or a top-five list in chat. Research does not authorize contacting universities, submitting applications, paying fees, or uploading candidate documents.
