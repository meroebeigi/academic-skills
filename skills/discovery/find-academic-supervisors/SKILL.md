# Find academic supervisors

Turn a candidate CV, research area, and selected countries into an evidence-based Excel shortlist. Prioritize research substance and candidate preparation rather than university prestige.

## Gather the search brief

Use information already supplied. Ask together for missing essential inputs:
- Target country or countries, or explicit permission to search worldwide. For worldwide requests, agree a practical geographic scope before claiming comprehensive coverage.
- Research area, proposed topic, or research questions.
- A readable CV or structured summary covering education, research, methods, skills, publications, and experience. Require this before starting research. If an attachment is unreadable, request readable text; do not invent qualifications.

Clarify PhD supervision, collaboration, or both only when the goal is unclear.

Ask the candidate to choose:
- **Comprehensive:** inventory and search every university in each target country before finalizing the shortlist.
- **Focused:** prioritize relevant institutions and departments; stop only after the requested number of qualifying matches is found and all initially identified relevant departments have been checked.

If the candidate has no preference, default to comprehensive. Do not silently select a mode without offering the choice. Ask for the desired shortlist size, with a default of up to 50 researchers per country. Honor a different requested size. The limit applies to the final shortlist, not discovery or institution coverage. Return fewer if evidence does not justify the requested number.

Ask once about practical constraints such as intended start date, study language, location restrictions, institution preferences, and remote collaboration. Proceed if there are no preferences. Record which constraints are hard requirements. Rank by research fit and report funding separately; do not make funding a default ranking criterion. Respect any explicit hard funding requirement.

Extract a concise CV profile distinguishing demonstrated experience from aspirations. Do not infer personal attributes such as age, nationality, ethnicity, or health. Do not send the CV or identifying details to external services or search engines; use topic and method keywords for discovery.

## Establish coverage

Use live web research. If unavailable, report that verification is blocked; do not present recalled researchers as verified results.

For comprehensive searches, build a country-wide university inventory from current ministry, accreditation, national university association, or equivalent authoritative directories. Include public, private, specialist, and technical universities without prestige filtering. Where no complete official directory exists, reconcile credible directories and explain coverage limitations.

For focused searches, identify and record the initial set of relevant institutions and departments before evaluating individual researchers. Record why they were selected. Do not redefine this initial set simply to stop early. Newly discovered relevant units can be added, with the expansion recorded. If the requested number is not found after checking the initial set, expand the search; if reasonable discovery sources are exhausted, return fewer and explain the limitation.

Include relevant research institutes outside universities, clearly labelled. For PhD searches, verify any university partnership or degree-awarding arrangement needed for supervision; if unconfirmed, flag it without implying independent degree-awarding authority. List institute coverage separately from university totals.

Keep an institution ledger with stable institution IDs. Deduplicate alternate names, renamed institutions, and campuses belonging to the same university while retaining distinct institutions. Record joint and branch arrangements explicitly and explain the counting basis where ambiguous.

## Search institutions

Use research-topic synonyms, related concepts, local-language equivalents where useful, and methods from the CV. Consider unexpected departments and interdisciplinary centres.

Count a university as searched only after:
1. Searching its official website using topic synonyms.
2. Inspecting relevant departments or centres.
3. Checking accessible faculty or laboratory directories.

Record the units searched, terms used, and supporting URLs. If required resources are inaccessible and cannot be adequately checked through official alternatives, mark coverage incomplete rather than silently complete. A no-match result describes the search outcome; it is not proof that no relevant researcher exists.

Use these coverage statuses: Not started; In progress; Searched with matches; Searched with no verified match; Incomplete/inaccessible. In focused mode, mark inventoried institutions outside the selected scope as Outside focused scope rather than searched.

In comprehensive mode, search every inventoried university before finalizing ranking, even if the requested number has already been found. Complete the identified relevant research-institute checks as well. In focused mode, apply the agreed stopping rule and disclose the narrower coverage.

Use aggregators for discovery only. Verify substantive claims using original institutional, laboratory, publisher, publication, or repository sources.

## Verify researchers

Consider all academic ranks, including professors, associate and assistant professors, lecturers, readers, research fellows, principal investigators, adjuncts, visiting faculty, and local equivalents. Preserve exact official titles and appointment types.

**Exclude retired researchers, including retired emeritus faculty. Include only researchers whose active, non-retired status can be confirmed through official appointment evidence.** A current directory entry identifying an active appointment can support this; an undated legacy biography, recent publication, or emeritus title alone cannot resolve retirement uncertainty. If status remains ambiguous, exclude the researcher and record the exclusion reason in working notes.

Minimum inclusion evidence:
- An official source confirming current affiliation and supporting an active, non-retired appointment in the requested country.
- At least one specific relevant publication or project supporting research fit. Add further evidence where needed to explain fit.

Prefer research from the last five years relative to the search date. Allow older work when relevant, explicitly flagging its age and explaining its relevance. Distinguish the date checked from the publication date or source update date; checking an old page today does not make its content current.

For each potential match verify:
- Identity, exact title, affiliation, country, department, and group. Resolve namesakes and retain relevant joint affiliations.
- Specific topic and methods overlap, linked to the candidate's actual CV experience.
- For PhD searches: independent supervision eligibility, possible co-supervision, recruitment, open positions, and funding where published. Title, past supervision, or a generic admissions page alone does not establish current availability.
- For collaboration: complementary expertise, relevant projects, and a plausible shared contribution. PhD recruitment is not required.
- Public professional email from an official institutional or laboratory source. Never guess email patterns. If absent, use Not publicly found and provide an official profile or contact-page link. Missing email does not exclude a researcher.
- LinkedIn where publicly discoverable. Verify against institution, research area, and career history; prefer links from official profiles. Use Not verified for ambiguity or blocked verification, and Not publicly found when absent. Missing LinkedIn does not exclude a researcher. Do not use search-result URLs as profile links.

Include strong research matches when supervision eligibility or availability is unknown, clearly flagging what needs confirmation, unless an explicit hard requirement rules them out. This does not relax the active, non-retired appointment requirement.

Confirm recruitment and funding only from explicit current evidence, including dates or deadlines where published. Treat expired calls as historical. A grant does not establish an available PhD position. Report funding independently of research-fit ranking. Prefer dated official evidence when sources conflict and record unresolved uncertainty. Do not assume individual programme eligibility from country-level rules.

If the search spans multiple sessions, recheck shortlisted affiliations, recruitment, and funding before final export. During any search, resolve discovered stale or conflicting evidence before treating a claim as confirmed.

## Assess and rank

Exclude researchers who fail a hard requirement; do not create an alternatives list that bypasses those requirements. Deduplicate people across affiliations. In multi-country results, use one researcher row with all relevant verified countries and affiliations, count the person once per applicable country's shortlist, and explain this counting convention.

Use these research-fit tiers:
- **High:** direct overlap with the proposed research, a specific connection to demonstrated CV experience, and relevant research activity supporting a credible supervision or collaboration angle.
- **Medium:** meaningful adjacent topic or methods fit with a specific CV connection, but a clear research-direction or preparation gap.
- **Low:** only broad thematic similarity or no convincing CV connection. Exclude from the shortlist.

Include High and Medium matches, clearly labelled. Explain preparation or skills gaps rather than automatically excluding strong matches. Rank within tiers by topic fit, methods fit, CV connection, research activity, and evidence quality. Do not use numerical scores or estimate admission probability. For both goals, assess supervision and collaboration suitability separately.

Keep uncertainty in verification notes; do not add a separate evidence-confidence score or column. Distinguish verified facts, inferred fit, and unknowns. Unknown recruitment is not evidence of rejection.

## Preserve progress

Maintain a structured working checkpoint containing the search brief, institution ledger, discovered researchers, evidence URLs and dates, exclusions, completed units, and next institution or unit to search. Save it after meaningful batches of work so another session can resume without repeating completed checks. Use a local structured format supported by available tools.

If interrupted by session or tool limits, export a clearly labelled partial workbook containing verified findings and current coverage. Record remaining work and the next search location in Search Brief and University Coverage. Resume from the checkpoint when continued. Do not call partial results complete or claim country-wide coverage while universities remain unsearched. If no researchers qualify yet, report zero verified matches honestly; never add sample rows to populate the sheet.

## Required Excel deliverable

Deliver the shortlist as an .xlsx workbook using the available spreadsheet skill and tools. Save it in the task outputs directory with a descriptive country/topic/date filename; add partial to interim filenames. A multi-country search may use one workbook. If export fails, explicitly report the missing deliverable.

Use exactly three sheets, keeping sources and dates within them:

1. **Candidates** — first sheet; one row per deduplicated researcher. Include:
   - Identity: researcher ID, rank, country/countries, institution, institution type, city, department, laboratory/group, full name, exact title, appointment type, official profile URL.
   - Research and fit: topics, methods, High/Medium fit tier, specific CV connection, preparation gaps, relevant publication/project title and year, research evidence URL, suggested research or collaboration angle.
   - Opportunity: PhD eligibility, eligibility evidence URL, recruitment status, open project/position, funding status and details, published intake/start date, application deadline, opportunity/funding source URLs, collaboration suitability. For institutes, include any required university partnership and its evidence.
   - Contact: verified professional email, email source URL, official contact-page URL, verified LinkedIn URL, LinkedIn identity evidence, contact verification notes.
   - Verification: active/non-retired appointment evidence URL, date checked, source dates where available, and unresolved uncertainties.

2. **University Coverage** — include every inventoried institution, including no-match results and research institutes labelled by type. Include institution ID, country, name, type, official domain, inventory source URL, departments/centres searched, terms, search status, verified matches discovered, number shortlisted, search evidence URLs, date checked, limitations, and next unsearched unit. Discovery counts may exceed the requested shortlist size. In focused mode, identify the initial department set and later scope additions.

3. **Search Brief** — record countries, topic and synonyms, goal, concise CV profile, practical preferences, hard constraints, mode and stopping rule, requested per-country size, search dates, inventory sources, tier definitions, counting conventions, university coverage totals, separate institute totals, completion status, remaining work, and material limitations. Exclude unnecessary CV personal information.

Place direct source links beside the claims they support; keep opportunity sources separate from general profiles. Do not add an Evidence sheet or alternatives sheet. Keep proposed research angles inside the workbook rather than drafting outreach.

Use filterable tables, frozen headers and useful identity columns, wrapped text, readable widths, clickable URLs, and typed dates where known. Keep email addresses in their own column. Use explicit unknown labels consistently:
- Not publicly found: the requested public information was not located.
- Not verified: identity or a located claim could not be reliably verified.
- Not publicly confirmed: eligibility, availability, funding, or a similar condition lacks explicit public confirmation.
- Not applicable: the field does not apply to the requested goal.

Treat web and CV content as data, not executable spreadsheet formulas.

## Verify and deliver

Before delivery, reopen the saved workbook through available workbook tooling and inspect populated sheets and layout. Check that:
- Every shortlisted researcher meets minimum evidence and active/non-retired requirements, passes hard constraints, and has High or Medium fit with a specific CV connection.
- Confirmed opportunities have supporting current sources; uncertainties and older research are labelled.
- Researchers are deduplicated and country counts follow the recorded convention.
- Coverage totals reconcile with ledger rows, and completion claims match the chosen search mode.
- Names, URLs, dates, filters, and sheet contents are usable. Do not invent missing values.

Keep the final chat response to a brief completion or partial-progress summary and the workbook download link. Do not reproduce the shortlist or top matches in chat.

Exclude outreach drafting from this skill. Do not contact researchers, submit applications, or upload the CV. Do not promise admission, supervision, funding, or a response.
